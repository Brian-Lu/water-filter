3. The spot

  • Unfinished basement, concrete floor: 40" wide × 36" deep × 80" high (floor to overhead pipe run). Homeowner measurement, please confirm.
  • Row in line: three housings on one shared bracket (~25") + ~4" for union/valve + 9" resin tank ≈ 38" of the 40". Brine tank in front of the resin tank; UV horizontal above the pipe run.
  • If 38" won't go with real fittings, move the sediment housing upstream on the main (frees ~9").
  • Run the row in 1" (copper or PEX) even if the house line is 3/4"; full-port ball valves + unions both sides of each device, one bypass loop around the whole row.
  • Cartridges change from below: keep ≥5" clear under each housing bowl.

4. Confirm on the site visit

5. Install-day asks

  • Valves + unions on every device, bypass loop around the row, softener on its own bypass too.
  • Pressure test and leak check before leaving; flush carbon cartridges to drain per Pentair (fines).
  • Leak sensor with auto-shutoff valve on the main.
  • Label each valve (bypass, in, out) and the drain line.
  • Show the homeowner one cartridge change (bowl down, O-ring grease, burp air).
  • Softener set from a measured hardness/chlorine test on the day (~3–4 grains expected).

Putting the row together (cross-unit rules from the manuals)
  1. Order is fixed: sediment → LR-BB50 → FloPlus → softener → UV. Viqua: a 5-µm sediment filter must precede the UV and the UV "should be the final piece of treatment equipment"; US Water: softener after the carbon stage (resin degrades above 2 ppm chlorine) and before the water heater.
  2. Shut-off valve before the row (Pentair requires one before each filter); valves + unions on both sides of every unit; one bypass around the whole row; the softener's included bypass stays on (US Water: "ALWAYS install the included bypass valve").
  3. Run the row in 1". All three Pentair caps are 1" NPT; the Matrixx valve is 1" (3/4" straights and a 1" elbow are supplied); US Water: outlet pipe must be the same size or larger than the supply.
  4. Pressure: everything is happy at 30–80 psi (Pentair 30–90, Matrixx 20–100 but PRV if daytime pressure is over 80). Cold water only, 40–100°F, no freezing, no direct sun.
  5. Grounding: if the copper main is the electrical ground, a #8 AWG (or larger) jumper around the plastic housings, clamped both ends per NEC (Pentair LR-BB50 manual, p.4).
  6. Support the housings on their brackets so the pipe carries no weight (Pentair); bracket top ≥34" off the floor so there is 5" under each bowl for cartridge changes (FloPlus manual, p.4).
  7. Thread sealant: tape on pipe-fitting threads is fine everywhere, but NOT on the Matrixx tank or valve threads (voids the warranty). Don't solder with the Viqua or the softener valve attached (O-rings/plastic).
  8. Electrical: two 120 V receptacles at the row, the UV one GFCI (Viqua: grounded, no adapters or extension cords); the softener outlet within 6 ft (its transformer cord is 6 ft). Third outlet under the kitchen sink for the RO.
  9. Drain: softener drain line 5/8" OD (1/2" ID) to the laundry sink with a ≥1.5" air gap, no direct connection; overhead run OK if no dips, falling 1/2" per 10' toward the drain.
  10. Start-up order: fill and flush each Pentair housing (15 min LR-BB50, 5 min FloPlus at 5 gpm) → softener start-up cycle (p.32) and salt → sanitize the whole system with household bleach per Viqua §3.2, then light the UV → RO under the sink last.
  11. Pressure-test and leak-check, then show the homeowner one Big Blue cartridge change (steps below).
Sediment housing: Pentek 150233 + DGD-5005-20 cartridge
  • Spec sheet: polypropylene 20" Big Blue housing, HFPP cap with 1" NPT in/out and a pressure-relief button on the inlet side; 23.22" × 7.45"; 40–100°F; max 90 psi; Buna-N O-ring; NSF/ANSI 42 (structural). Takes any 4.5" × 20" cartridge.
  • Pentair publishes no stand-alone install sheet for the bare housing. Install it exactly like the FloPlus (same housing, same bracket pattern): tape threads, adapters hand-started then wrench-tight, "IN" toward the meter, two wrenches on the unions, bracket top ≥34" off the floor.
  • Bracket 150061 (or the plumber's shared 3-housing bracket); the housing wrench supplied with the two Pentair kits fits it, so one wrench serves all three.
  • Cartridge DGD-5005-20 (dual-gradient 50→5 µm): change every 3–6 months or when pressure drops noticeably (Pentair's general cartridge guidance). Same change procedure as the LR-BB50 below.
  • Housing bottom (bowl) should be replaced every 10 years (opaque); date it at install (Pentair caution, both manuals).
Lead / cyst: Pentair LR-BB50 (cartridge LR-FB20BB, #655210-43)

Specs (manual p.4, spec sheet): 1" NPT molded into the head (3/4" reducer bushings included); 10 gpm; 30–90 psi; 40–100°F; cold water only; rated capacity 35,000 gal on the spec sheet (the manual's data sheet says 40,000; plan on 35,000). Kit includes cartridge, wrench, mounting bracket and screws. NSF/ANSI 53 lead (pH 6.5 and 8.5) and cyst, CSA B483.1.

Install (manual pp. 4–5):

  1. Shut-off valve before the filter; install after the water meter. Allow at least 1-1/2" under the housing (manual minimum; we want 5" like the FloPlus).
  2. About three wraps of plumber's tape, clockwise, on each fitting; start adapters into the cap by hand, then wrench firm, do not over-tighten.
  3. Measure across the assembled fittings and subtract 1-1/2" for 1" pipe (1" for 3/4" pipe); mark the pipe to remove.
  4. Shut the main, open the lowest faucet to drain, cut the pipe, file the ends square; bucket under.
  5. Set the assembly with the cap's "IN" facing the incoming supply; spread the pipe ends; two adjustable wrenches, one holding the adapter, one on the nut; repeat on the outlet.
  6. Open the water slowly, press the red relief button to burp air, check for leaks, open the nearest faucet and flush 15 minutes.
  7. Jumper wire (#8 AWG min.) around the filter if the pipes ground the house; support the filter's weight so it doesn't stress the pipe.

Cartridge change (manual pp. 6–7): turn the cap's bypass handle clockwise, press the red relief button, bucket under, unscrew the bowl with the wrench, lift out the big O-ring and wipe it, discard the cartridge, rinse the bowl, lubricate the O-ring with clean silicone grease and seat it level in its groove, drop in the new cartridge over the standpipe, hand-tighten the bowl (snug, not wrenched), open water slowly, press the relief button, flush 15 minutes, check for drips. A drip at the bowl = reseat the O-ring; a drip at a fitting = shut off, relieve pressure, tighten about 1/2 turn. Pentair customer service 800-279-9404.

PFAS / chlorine: Pentair FloPlus Protect 20BB (cartridge #655230-43)

Specs (manual p.3): 30–90 psi; 5 gpm; 40–100°F; 40,000 gal. System includes housing, cartridge, mounting bracket and housing wrench. Tools: pencil, two adjustable wrenches, file, screwdriver, pipe cutter or hacksaw. Galvanized fittings must be used on galvanized pipe. Cold water only; not in direct sunlight.

Install (manual p.4):

  1. Load the cartridge into the housing first (per the replacement steps), then install after the meter.
  2. Shut the cold supply and open the nearest faucet to relieve pressure.
  3. Assemble fittings into the cap by hand, then wrench-firm (no over-tightening). Measure across the fittings, subtract 1-1/2" for 1" pipe, mark and cut the main, thread/tape the ends.
  4. Cap "IN" toward the incoming supply; two wrenches on the inlet adapter, then the outlet.
  5. Jumper wire if the pipes ground the electrical system.
  6. Screw the bracket to the cap, bracket to the wall, top of bracket no less than 34" off the floor so there is 5" below the housing.
  7. Fill slowly, press the red relief button, flush 5 minutes at 5 gpm before use, check for leaks before leaving.

Cartridge change (manual pp. 4–5): shut off, press the relief button, bucket, bowl off (turn clockwise looking up), remove and wipe the O-ring, discard the cartridge, rinse the bowl, 1/3 water + 2 tablespoons bleach, scrub with a non-abrasive sponge, rinse; silicone-grease the O-ring and seat it level; new cartridge over the standpipe; bowl hand-tight; water on slowly; relief button; flush 5 minutes at 5 gpm. If shutting the filter off also kills the house water, fill a bucket first. Bowl replaced every 10 years.

Softener: US Water Systems Matrixx

Requirements (manual pp. 3–4, 12): indoor 120 V grounded outlet within 6 ft (12 V transformer with a 6-ft cord, use only the included one); 20 psi minimum, 100 psi maximum, PRV if daytime pressure is over 80 psi; water under 100°F; install before the water heater (10' or more of pipe between them); outside faucets and irrigation stay on hard water before the softener; as close to the meter and a drain as practical; no freezing, no direct sun; place where a leak does the least damage.

Plumbing (pp. 4, 17–19): copper, brass or PEX (lead-free solder); plumbing the same size as the valve, outlet no smaller than the supply; always install the included bypass (or a 3-valve hard-piped bypass); the bypass is arrowed for flow direction; supplied fittings are 3/4" straights and a 1" elbow; tape or pipe sealant on the plumbing fitting threads only, never on the tank or valve threads (voids the warranty); secure all piping so the bypass carries no stress; the supplied flex connectors seal on gaskets and need no tape. Tools listed: channel locks, screwdriver, Teflon tape, razor knife, two adjustable wrenches.

Drain (p.19): 5/8" OD / 1/2" ID drain line clamped to the valve, run to the laundry tub, floor drain or approved air-gap fitting with a physical air gap of at least 1.5"; no direct connection; may run overhead with no dips or kinks; a long horizontal run should be hard pipe of larger diameter falling 1/2" per 10'. A second 5/8" line for the brine-tank overflow fitting is optional; cheap insurance, run it to the same sink.

Prep and start-up (pp. 13, 26–32): resin and carbon may ship separately and are loaded into the tank on site (p.13, "Resin Installation"). Start-up: with the bypass in bypass, start an immediate regeneration; when the valve reaches backwash, crack the bypass about 1/8 turn so the tank fills slowly (knocking = too fast), open fully once water reaches the drain, let it backwash the air out, skip brine draw (hold Set/Change, or "Go to next regen step" in the Legacy View app), run the full rapid rinse until the drain runs clear, run the full refill to prime the brine line, then it returns to service. Fill the brine tank with extra-coarse or solar salt (99.8% pure). Program hardness from the day-of test (buttons p.27 or the Legacy View app p.28).

UV: Viqua VH410-V

Requirements (manual §1 safety, §3.1): plug into a grounded receptacle protected by a GFCI; no adapters, no extension cords; indoor, cold line only, before any branch lines, last treatment step; 5-µm sediment filter upstream; shut off water and release pressure before any service. Metal fittings and at least 10" of copper pipe on the outlet (heat and UV degrade plastic). Lamp contains mercury: never vacuum a broken lamp.

Mounting (§3.1): vertical with the inlet at the bottom and the lamp connector on top is preferred; horizontal is allowed only with the outlet port pointing up so air purges. Leave clearance equal to the chamber's own length at the lamp end to pull the lamp and quartz sleeve. Controller mounted above or beside the chamber, horizontally, with drip loops in every cord. Wall clamps are supplied; union connectors recommended; do not solder with the chamber connected (O-rings). Optional: flow restrictor on the outlet (direction-arrowed), bypass assembly, solenoid valve.

Start-up (§3.2): the whole downstream system, including the water heater, must be sanitized by flushing with household bleach before the UV is put in service. Inspect for water on the controller or lamp connector before plugging in.

Service (§4): lamp rated ~9,000 hours → replace annually with a genuine Viqua lamp (S410RL-HO), then reset the lamp timer (§5.1.3); quartz sleeve cleaned with CLR or vinegar when the UV-intensity reading drops, replaced if it won't come clean; clean the UV sensor at the same time (§4.3). Lamp change is a no-tools job; see the Viqua videos linked above.

Kitchen RO: Waterdrop G3P800 (tankless, 800 GPD)

Install (manual pp. 6–11, steps 1–8):

  1. Feed-water adapter on the cold line under the sink (cold only).
  2. RO faucet in its own hole (1-1/4"), with the small UV sterilizer pushed onto the faucet stem (output end, the one without the lamp ring) and locked with the clip.
  3. Drain saddle on the sink drain pipe with the foam seal aligned to the hole; pick a spot that's easy to reach.
  4. Stand the housing in the cabinet: 18.12" × 5.67" × 17.72", so ~19" clear height.
  5. Tubing (1/4" PE, push-fit, insert to the mark on the tube, lock clip on): feed adapter → INPUT; FILTERED → UV sterilizer → faucet; drain saddle → WASTE.
  6. Power: faucet cord → FAUCET socket, UV cord → UV STERILIZER socket (keyed, won't go in wrong), power adapter → POWER port, then the wall outlet under the sink.
  7. Install the three filters (CF, CB, RO) and start up / flush per step 8 before drinking.

Operation and upkeep (pp. 13–18): TDS readout on the faucet, expect 90%+ rejection after the initial flush; the UV ring lights only while the faucet runs; the unit self-flushes 20 s after every 2 hours of run time and after a power cut. Filter lives: CF up to 6 months / 800 gal, CB 12 months / 1,600 gal, RO membrane 24 months / 3,200 gal (life reminder on the display; swap guide p.15). Waterdrop support 1-888-352-3558.
