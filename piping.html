Piping schematic and connection details

Companion to the plan and the to-scale drawings. Flow runs right to left, as in the basement; the bay is 65" × 36" × 80" with the laundry sink moved out. Last updated 10 Nov 2026. Pipe sizes, clearances and heights come from the manufacturer manuals (plan §6); code references are to the 2020 Plumbing Code of New York State (PCNYS) and the 2017 NEC as adopted in NY. The plumber owns the final routing; anything marked "confirm" is to be checked on site.

1. Schematic: what connects to what

Swipe sideways on a phone. Tags (V1, U1, PG1…) match the fittings list in §3.

Sheet 1. V1/V2 isolate the whole row; V3 is the bypass (normally closed, handle tagged). Unions U1–U6 let any unit come out without cutting pipe. T1 splits the flow across the two FloPlus housings and T2 rejoins it; the two jumpers are the same length so each housing sees half the flow (≤ 5 gpm, the limit of its PFAS listing), and there are no valves on the branches (closing one would push the whole flow through the other). U7/U8 let either FloPlus head come out alone. PG1/PG2 show the pressure drop across the four cartridges. BD1 gives a raw-water sample; BD2 flushes the carbon fines to the standpipe before the softener and UV ever see them. The LR-BB50's own cap bypass lets that cartridge be changed with the rest of the row in service.

4. Details

Softener drain, brine and power

Sheet 3. The Pentair valve pushes 2.7 gpm to drain for a few minutes during each regeneration, so the line is clamped at the valve's 3/4" NPT drain fitting and secured along its run (tubing is not in the box). It may rise and run along the joists (no sags, ≤ 25 ft), then drop into a 2" trapped standpipe made from the old laundry-sink drain, the end held ≥ 1.5" above the rim by an air-gap fitting. No hard connection to the drain. With water this soft the softener may go weeks between regenerations: set the calendar override to ≤ 14 days so the standpipe trap stays wet. Brine-tank overflow is gravity only and must fall the whole way.

UV chamber

Sheet 4. Viqua prefers vertical (inlet bottom, lamp on top); horizontal is allowed with the outlet pointing up so the chamber purges air. Use unions, metal fittings and ≥ 10" of copper on the outlet; mount the controller level with drip loops in the cords; GFCI receptacle. Leave a chamber-length (24") free at the lamp end.

Kitchen RO (under the sink)

Sheet 5 (drawn for the Waterdrop G3P800). Cold feed only. Three 1/4" lines: feed → INPUT, FILTERED → UV sterilizer → faucet, WASTE → drain. The faucet cord and UV cord plug into the back of the unit; the adapter needs a receptacle in the cabinet. If the homeowner goes with the Brondell Capella RC250 instead: same feed adapter and drain saddle, no power, its reservoir is built into the unit, and its faucet is an air-gap faucet, so the drain line runs up to the faucet base and back down to the saddle (satisfies PCNYS 611.2 as delivered).

5. Code and best-practice notes

  1. Valves. Full-open valves on the discharge side of the meter and on the water-heater supply (PCNYS 606.1); shutoff to each appliance (606.2). The row adds V1/V2 plus the bypass; the softener keeps its own bypass as well (Pentair ships one; keep it in).
  2. Pressure. Static over 80 psi requires an ASSE 1003 PRV set ≤ 80 (604.8). Everything in the row is rated 90 psi or more except that the Pentair housings want ≤ 90 (the Pentair softener is rated 20–125 psi, 60 optimum). A PRV or any check valve makes a thermal expansion tank mandatory at the heater (607.3).
  3. Treatment-equipment discharge. Waste and overflow from filters and softeners must end in an air gap (608.4); indirect waste into a receptor needs an air gap of at least twice the pipe's effective opening (802.3.1). The softener drain now ends in a 2" trapped standpipe built on the old laundry-sink drain, rim 18–42" above its trap weir (802.4.3).
  4. What stays raw. Irrigation and hose bibs stay upstream of the softener (Pentair manual §6, and it saves cartridges). Laundry is currently upstream too; re-routing its tee to after V2 is optional (soft water for the washer).
  5. Order. 5 µm sediment first, UV last (Viqua); softener after the carbon stage (chlorine shortens resin life); softener before the water heater and not on a short run to it (Pentair manual §7: hot water must not back up into the softener; a check valve only as a last resort).
  6. Grounding. If the copper main is the grounding electrode, a bonding jumper around the plastic housings and softener is required and must let equipment be removed (NEC 250.68(B)); size per Table 250.66 (#4 Cu for 2/0–3/0 service conductors, #8 for 2 AWG or smaller). Pentair's manuals call for #8 minimum. Keep NEC 110.26 working space (30" × 36" × 78") in front of the panel clear, which is why the row is down the main, not at it.
  7. RO drain. PCNYS 611.1 requires an NSF 58 unit (both candidates are listed); 611.2 requires the RO discharge to enter the drain through an air gap or an NSF 58 air-gap device. The Brondell RC250 ships with an air-gap faucet and meets this as delivered. The Waterdrop G3P800 ships a non-air-gap faucet and a drain saddle, which is how most of these are installed; with it, a strict reading of 611.2 wants an air-gap device in the waste line. Homeowner decision, plumber confirms with the Village inspector.
  8. Materials. Copper (type L) or PEX with 1" fittings, lead-free solder/flux (PCNYS 605); PTFE tape on fitting threads; never point a torch at the softener valve (thermoplastic) and never sweat pipe with water in it (steam damages the valve — Pentair); no soldering with the UV connected.
  9. Start-up order. Fill and burp each housing, flush LR-BB50 15 min and FloPlus 5 min through BD2; softener start-up cycle and salt; bleach-sanitize the downstream piping, then light the UV; RO last. Pressure-test and leak-check; label V1/V2/V3 and the drain line.
