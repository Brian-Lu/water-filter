Piping schematic and connection details

Companion to the plan and the to-scale drawings. Flow runs right to left, as in the basement. Pipe sizes, clearances and heights come from the manufacturer manuals (plan §6); code references are to the 2020 Plumbing Code of New York State (PCNYS) and the 2017 NEC as adopted in NY. The plumber owns the final routing; anything marked "confirm" is to be checked on site.

1. Schematic: what connects to what

Swipe sideways on a phone. Tags (V1, U1, PG1…) match the fittings list in §3.

Sheet 1. V1/V2 isolate the whole row; V3 is the bypass (normally closed, handle tagged). Unions U1–U6 let any unit come out without cutting pipe. PG1/PG2 show the pressure drop across the three cartridges. BD1 gives a raw-water sample; BD2 flushes the carbon fines to the laundry sink before the softener and UV ever see them. The LR-BB50's own cap bypass lets that cartridge be changed with the rest of the row in service.

2. Dimensioned elevation (to scale, 1 px = 0.1")

Sheet 2. Solid blue = treated-water line in flow order; dashed blue = the bypass loop (V3), which carries nothing in normal service and rejoins the house line after V2. Header centreline at 58" puts the Big Blue cap tops at ≈ 60" and the lowest bowl (LR-BB50, 27.2" tall) at ≈ 33" off the floor, far more than the 5" Pentair needs for a cartridge change and still reachable without a step. The softener's ports sit lower (≈ 52", confirm on the unit), so the FloPlus outlet drops 6" in the 5" gap. The UV hangs horizontally above the housings at 68" with the outlet port up (Viqua's rule for horizontal mounting) and 24" of open air to its right for the lamp pull; its controller mounts level, beside it. Everything stays below the 80" pipe run. Horizontal centrelines from the left edge of the bay: softener 6.5", FloPlus 19.8", LR-BB50 28", sediment 36.25" (1" between housings, 5" for the union/valve between FloPlus and softener), 38" used of 40".

4. Details

Softener drain, brine and power

Sheet 3. The valve pushes ~2 gpm to drain for a few minutes during each regeneration, so the line is clamped at the barb and secured along its run. It may rise and run along the joists (no sags), then drop to the sink, with the end held ≥ 1.5" above the flood rim by a clip. No hard connection to the drain. Overflow from the brine tank is gravity only and must fall the whole way.

UV chamber

Sheet 4. Viqua prefers vertical (inlet bottom, lamp on top); horizontal is allowed with the outlet pointing up so the chamber purges air. Use unions, metal fittings and ≥ 10" of copper on the outlet; mount the controller level with drip loops in the cords; GFCI receptacle. Leave a chamber-length (24") free at the lamp end.

Kitchen RO (under the sink)

Sheet 5. Cold feed only. Three 1/4" lines: feed → INPUT, FILTERED → UV sterilizer → faucet, WASTE → drain. The faucet cord and UV cord plug into the back of the unit; the adapter needs a receptacle in the cabinet.

5. Code and best-practice notes

  1. Valves. Full-open valves on the discharge side of the meter and on the water-heater supply (PCNYS 606.1); shutoff to each appliance (606.2). The row adds V1/V2 plus the bypass; the softener keeps its own bypass as well (US Water: "always install the included bypass").
  2. Pressure. Static over 80 psi requires an ASSE 1003 PRV set ≤ 80 (604.8). Everything in the row is rated 90 psi or more except that Pentair wants ≤ 90 and US Water ≤ 100 (80 by day). A PRV or any check valve makes a thermal expansion tank mandatory at the heater (607.3).
  3. Treatment-equipment discharge. Waste and overflow from filters and softeners must end in an air gap (608.4); indirect waste into a receptor needs an air gap of at least twice the pipe's effective opening (802.3.1). A standpipe, if used instead of the sink, is 18–42" above its trap weir (802.4.3).
  4. What stays raw. Irrigation and hose bibs stay upstream of the softener (US Water, and it saves cartridges). Laundry is currently upstream too; re-routing its tee to after V2 is optional (soft water for the washer).
  5. Order. 5 µm sediment first, UV last (Viqua); softener after the carbon stage because chlorine over 2 ppm shortens resin life (US Water); softener before the water heater with ≥ 10 ft of pipe between (US Water).
  6. Grounding. If the copper main is the grounding electrode, a bonding jumper around the plastic housings and softener is required and must let equipment be removed (NEC 250.68(B)); size per Table 250.66 (#4 Cu for 2/0–3/0 service conductors, #8 for 2 AWG or smaller). Pentair's manuals call for #8 minimum. Keep NEC 110.26 working space (30" × 36" × 78") in front of the panel clear, which is why the row is down the main, not at it.
  7. RO drain. PCNYS 611.1 requires an NSF 58 unit (the G3P800 is); 611.2 requires the RO discharge to enter the drain through an air gap or an NSF 58 air-gap device. Waterdrop ships a non-air-gap faucet and a drain saddle, which is how most of these are installed; a strict reading of 611.2 wants an air-gap faucet or device in the waste line. Homeowner decision, plumber confirms with the Village inspector.
  8. Materials. Copper (type L) or PEX with 1" fittings, lead-free solder/flux (PCNYS 605); PTFE tape on fitting threads but never on the softener tank/valve threads (voids the warranty); no soldering within 6" of the softener valve or with the UV connected.
  9. Start-up order. Fill and burp each housing, flush LR-BB50 15 min and FloPlus 5 min through BD2; softener start-up cycle and salt; bleach-sanitize the downstream piping, then light the UV; RO last. Pressure-test and leak-check; label V1/V2/V3 and the drain line.
