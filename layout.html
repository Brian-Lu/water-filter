Basement layout, drawn to scale

Space: 65" wide × 36" deep × 80" high (concrete floor to the overhead pipe run) once the laundry sink is moved out; unfinished area along the main. Equipment sizes are from the manufacturers' spec sheets; 10 drawing units = 1". Flow runs right to left, as in the basement: meter → sediment → LR-BB50 → two FloPlus in parallel → softener → UV → house. Valve tags and the fittings list are on piping.html; the equipment list is on index.html. Last updated 10 Nov 2026.

Verdict: the softener and both FloPlus housings fit in the bay, but tight.

  • Width: 62.7" used of 65", so 2.3" spare. This only works with the housings joined by close nipples, as piping.html already specifies. There is no room for a union between adjacent housings.
  • Depth: the brine tank stands in front of the resin tank and reaches 34.5" of the 36".
  • Height: the top pipe is at 75.6", 4.4" under the 80" cap. The header drops from 58" to 54" to make room for the parallel jumpers.
  • Yearly service needs no workaround: every bowl drops straight down with 28" or more below it, the UV lamp pulls into open air, and salt goes in from the front.

If the bay measures under about 63.5" wall to wall, or under 35" deep at the floor, use the fallback (no softener), which has more than 13" to spare.

What this drawing assumes

  • Softener drawn: Pentair WaterTrust Pro 35974 (resin tank 10.2" dia × 51.6" tall including the valve, round brine tank 18.5" dia × 33.5"), the chosen unit (index.html §2).
  • The bay is 65" clear between side obstructions, with a mountable back wall across the full width and the front open.
  • The main arrives from the right at about 65" and the house line leaves to the left, as in piping.html. If the main is elsewhere, the inlet drop moves but the row does not.
  • The laundry sink was at the left of the old bay, so its drain connection is behind where the softener now stands. confirm
  • Fitting allowances are for 1" copper or brass: ball valve 4", union 2.5", tee 3", elbow 1.5" centre to face, male adapter 0.9" proud of the cap.

2. The parallel FloPlus pair

  • After the LR-BB50, tee T1 splits the flow. One side goes straight into FloPlus A. The other rises into jumper S, passes over A and drops into FloPlus B.
  • FloPlus A's outlet rises into jumper R, passes over B and comes down into tee T2, where B's outlet joins it.
  • Each path has one jumper of the same length (first in, last out), so the two housings see the same resistance and split the flow evenly. That is what keeps each one at or under its 5 gpm rating.
  • S and R have to cross once in the 6" gap. R is rolled back against the wall and S stays in the port plane, 2.9" apart.
  • Unions U7 and U8 sit on the jumpers, so either FloPlus head can come out without cutting pipe. The row-end unions from piping.html stay.
  • No valves on the individual branches. Closing one branch would push the whole flow through the other housing and exceed 5 gpm. Each FloPlus cap has its own bypass for cartridge changes.

3. Softener drain now that the laundry sink is gone

The softener discharges about 2.7 gpm for a few minutes per regeneration and must end in an air gap (PCNYS 608.4, 802.3.1). Pentair's manual asks for 1/2" ID tubing, an air gap, and a run of no more than 25 ft; neither the tubing nor the air-gap fitting is in the box.

6. For the plumber to confirm on site

  • Clear width at the wall, at 28–76" height. The primary needs 62.7"; under about 63.5" it does not fit.
  • Clear depth at the floor on the left. The tanks need 34.5". If less, the brine tank can turn to the front-right of the resin tank, which then covers the floor in front of FloPlus B.
  • Nothing below 80" in the bay other than the pipe run, and nothing on the wall between 54" and 76".
  • Where the main enters and at what height, and that the 11" inlet drop has room for V1, U1 and PG1/BD1. They can go on the main to the right of the bay instead, as in piping.html.
  • Softener port height and which port is the inlet. On the 35974 the inlet is the left port seen from the front, so with right-to-left flow the inlet and outlet lines cross behind the valve. Drawn as a 54" inlet run passing in front of the outlet riser.
  • UV port and lamp-end orientation against the Viqua manual: outlet up, lamp connector at the right-hand end.
  • The old sink's drain connection: location, size, trap and vent, and whether a standpipe there meets 802.4.3.
  • Blocking in the wall at 54–58" across 42" for the four-housing bracket line, and for the UV clamps at 69.5".
  • That each FloPlus and LR-BB50 bracket holds the cap with ports at the same height and depth as the Pentek 150233, so the close nipples line up.
  • Receptacles: two GFCI-protected outlets at the left, clear of any joint above them. Softener transformer within its cord length; UV controller mounted level with drip loops.
  • Bonding jumper around the whole row, as in piping.html. Pentair's softener manual also asks for it across the plastic valve.
  • Static pressure. Pentair gives 60 psi as the softener's optimum and 125 psi as its limit; the PRV rule in piping.html is unchanged.

7. Walk-through

  1. Water comes in at the top right at 65". A tee sends the bypass loop left along the wall (V3, normally closed) and the feed down through V1, U1 and PG1/BD1.
  2. At 54" it runs left through the sediment housing, a close nipple, and the LR-BB50.
  3. T1 splits it across FloPlus A and FloPlus B; T2 brings it back together. PG2/BD2 follow.
  4. It passes over the softener valve, drops into the softener inlet, and comes back up the outlet riser.
  5. The riser feeds the UV inlet from below. Water leaves the UV from the top at the right-hand end, turns back left at 75", passes V2, and goes to the water heater and house. The bypass loop rejoins just after V2.
